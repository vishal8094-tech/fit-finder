Why women's sizes vary so much

Women's sizing varies more between brands than almost any other category — a "M" in one label can equal a "S" or "L" elsewhere. Body shape, cut style (fitted vs relaxed) and regional sizing conventions all play a part. Measuring yourself is the most reliable starting point.

How to measure yourself

Bust

Wear a well-fitted, non-padded bra. Measure around the fullest part of your bust, keeping the tape level and snug but not tight.

Waist

Measure around the narrowest part of your natural waist, usually just above the navel.

Hips

Stand with feet together and measure around the fullest part of your hips and seat.

Tip: your bust, waist and hip measurements may fall into different size bands. When they do, use the size that fits the tightest area of the garment (e.g. bust for tops, hips for bottoms).

Fit preference matters too

  • Loose fit: size up for a relaxed silhouette, especially in structured fabrics.
  • Regular fit: go with your measured size for a balanced everyday fit.
  • Tight fit: size down only for stretch fabrics like jersey or lycra blends.

Frequently asked questions

How do I measure my bust size?

Measure around the fullest part of your bust while wearing a well-fitted, non-padded bra, keeping the tape level all the way around.

Why do UK and US women's sizes differ so much?

UK sizes generally run about one size larger in number than US sizes for the same measurements, and both differ from EU and India sizing conventions.

Should I go by bust, waist or hip measurement?

For tops and dresses, prioritise your bust measurement. For bottoms, prioritise waist and hip. If they fall in different size bands, size up and consider alterations.
