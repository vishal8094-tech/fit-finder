Why men's sizes vary by brand

A men's "L" from one brand can fit like a "M" from another. Sizing depends on the brand's block pattern, fabric stretch and country of origin. That's why measuring your own body — rather than trusting a label — gives the most reliable result.

How to measure yourself

Chest

Wrap the tape around the fullest part of your chest, passing under your arms and across your shoulder blades. Keep the tape level and snug, but don't pull it tight.

Waist

Measure around your natural waistline — usually just above the belly button — while standing relaxed, not sucked in.

Shoulders

Measure from the edge of one shoulder to the other, across your back, for a fitted shirt or jacket.

Tip: measure over light clothing, and take each measurement twice to confirm consistency before matching it to a size chart.

Fit preference matters too

  • Loose fit: add a size for extra room through the chest and sleeves.
  • Regular fit: go with your measured size for a balanced, everyday fit.
  • Tight fit: size down only if the fabric has stretch; otherwise stick to your measurement.

Frequently asked questions

How do I measure my chest size for men's clothing?

Wrap a measuring tape around the fullest part of your chest, under your arms and across your shoulder blades, keeping the tape level and snug but not tight.

What is the difference between US and EU men's sizes?

EU sizes typically run 1-2 sizes larger in number than US sizes for the same fit, since EU sizing is often based on chest circumference in centimetres.

Should I size up if I'm between two sizes?

If you prefer a relaxed fit or plan to layer, size up. For a fitted look, choose the smaller size and check the brand's specific size chart.
